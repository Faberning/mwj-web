If you have moved from South Africa to New Zealand on a work visa, the first question is usually not about lenders. It is about whether you are allowed to buy a home at all. The honest answer is that it depends on your visa. Under New Zealand's overseas investment rules, people on a temporary visa, such as a work visa, generally cannot buy an existing home without government consent. Once you hold a residence class visa and meet the "ordinarily resident" test, the picture changes, and getting a mortgage becomes a question of income, deposit and paperwork like it is for anyone else.

I work with a lot of South African families on the Hibiscus Coast and across Auckland. Here is how it usually works, and what I would get ready before you start looking at houses.

## Can you buy a house in New Zealand on a work visa?

In most cases, not an existing home, at least not straight away. The Overseas Investment Act treats most residential property as sensitive land. If you are not a New Zealand citizen or resident, or an Australian or Singaporean citizen, you usually need consent from the Overseas Investment Office before you can buy it. Holding a work visa, including the Accredited Employer Work Visa, does not by itself make you a resident for these rules.

There are some narrow exceptions, for example for certain new apartments in large developments, but they do not cover most family homes. The rules can change, so I always point people to the official guidance from Toitū Te Whenua Land Information New Zealand (LINZ) and recommend a property lawyer checks your position before you sign anything.

### What changes when you get residence?

Once you hold a residence class visa, you can buy a home without consent if you are "ordinarily resident" in New Zealand. That test looks at how long you have lived here and whether you are tax resident. Many South Africans plan their purchase around this point: they rent while their residence application is processed, save a deposit, and line up a pre-approval so they are ready to move once the visa is granted.

If your partner already holds residence or citizenship, the rules may apply differently to you as a couple. That is worth checking with a lawyer early, because it can change your timing.

## Will a bank lend to you before you have residence?

When you are allowed to buy, lenders then look at your visa as part of the risk. They want to know what type of visa you hold, how long is left on it, and whether you are on a pathway to residence. Some lenders are more comfortable than others with recent arrivals, and their appetite shifts over time.

This is one of the main reasons people come to me. A bank can only tell you its own policy. I compare 25+ bank and non-bank lenders, so I can tell you which ones currently look at your type of visa and situation, and which ones are unlikely to.

## How do lenders look at your income?

Lenders want to see stable New Zealand income. If you are employed here on a permanent contract, your payslips and employment agreement do most of the work. If you have only just started, some lenders want you past your trial period, and some will look at your work history in South Africa as supporting evidence.

Income still coming from South Africa, such as rent from a property you kept, is harder. Many New Zealand lenders give it little or no weight, or discount it heavily. If you are relying on it, we need to know early.

## Can you use money from South Africa for your deposit?

Often, yes, as long as you can show where it came from. Lenders and the anti-money laundering rules both require proof of the source of your deposit, so expect to provide statements that trace the money from your South African accounts to your New Zealand one.

Moving money out of South Africa has its own rules, including tax clearance from SARS for larger amounts. Allow time for this, because a deposit stuck in a transfer can hold up an offer. Your South African bank or a foreign exchange specialist can explain what applies to you. Exchange rates move too, so plan your deposit with some buffer rather than to the last rand.

### Can you use KiwiSaver or the Kāinga Ora First Home Loan?

Possibly, but not always while you are on a work visa. KiwiSaver membership and the first home withdrawal both have their own eligibility rules, and the Kāinga Ora First Home Loan is generally for New Zealand citizens and residents. I check what you qualify for rather than assume, because the answer often changes once residence comes through.

## What about your credit history?

Your South African credit record does not carry over. New Zealand lenders check New Zealand credit bureaus, so when you arrive you usually have a thin file or none at all. That is not a black mark, but it means lenders lean more on your bank statements and how you manage your money here.

A few habits help: open a New Zealand bank account and keep it in good order, pay bills on time, and be careful with buy now pay later and new credit cards in the months before you apply. Lenders see all of it.

## What should you prepare before you apply?

- Your passport and visa, including the expiry date and any residence application
- Your employment agreement and recent payslips
- Three to six months of New Zealand bank statements
- Statements tracing your deposit from South Africa, plus any tax clearance paperwork
- Details of any debts, in New Zealand and in South Africa
- If you are self-employed or contracting, your financial statements and tax returns

I give you a checklist at the start so nothing is missed.

## Key takeaways

- On a work visa you usually cannot buy an existing home without Overseas Investment Office consent. Check your position with LINZ guidance and a lawyer first.
- Residence class visa holders who are ordinarily resident in New Zealand can buy without consent.
- Lenders look at your visa type and pathway, your New Zealand income, the source of your deposit and your New Zealand credit history.
- Moving your deposit from South Africa takes time and paperwork, so start early.
- An adviser who knows lender policy for recent arrivals can save you a lot of trial and error.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can you get a mortgage if you have a visa?**

It depends on the visa. On a temporary visa, such as a work visa, you generally need government consent to buy an existing home, so the question of a mortgage often comes later. With a residence class visa, and once you are ordinarily resident, you can buy without consent, and lenders then assess you on your income, deposit and credit history.

**Do I need residence before I can buy a home in New Zealand?**

For most existing homes, yes, unless you get consent from the Overseas Investment Office or another exception applies. Many South Africans rent while their residence is processed and get ready to buy once it is granted.

**Can I use my South African savings for a deposit?**

Usually, yes, if you can show where the money came from. Expect to trace it from your South African accounts to New Zealand, and allow time for SARS tax clearance and the transfer.

**Does my South African credit history count in New Zealand?**

No. New Zealand lenders check local credit bureaus, so you start with a thin file. Keeping your New Zealand accounts in good order matters more.
